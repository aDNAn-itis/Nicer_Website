NICER Mission Details

Comprehensive information about NASA's Neutron star Interior Composition Explorer mission

Mission Overview

The Neutron star Interior Composition Explorer (NICER) is a NASA Astrophysics Mission of Opportunity within the Explorer program. Launched on June 3, 2017, aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket, NICER was installed on the International Space Station (ISS) as an external payload.

NICER represents a breakthrough in X-ray timing instrumentation, providing scientists with unprecedented capabilities to study neutron stars—the collapsed cores of massive stars that pack more mass than our Sun into a sphere only about 20 kilometers across.

Primary Mission Objective

Measure neutron star radii to within 5% precision to constrain the equation of state of matter at nuclear densities, providing insights into the fundamental physics of the universe's densest observable matter.

Technical Specifications

X-ray Timing Instrument

Detector Type: Silicon Drift Detectors (SDD)
Number of Detectors: 56
Energy Range: 0.2 - 12 keV
Energy Resolution: 85 eV @ 1 keV
Timing Resolution: ~100 ns
Effective Area: 1900 cm² @ 1.5 keV

X-ray Concentrators

Optic Type: Wolter-I Concentrators
Number of Optics: 56
Focal Length: 1.1 meters
Field of View: ~30 arcmin diameter
Angular Resolution: ~2.5 arcmin HPD
Coating: Gold

Platform & Operations

Host Platform: International Space Station
Installation: JEM-EF (External Facility)
Orbital Altitude: ~408 km
Orbital Period: 92.9 minutes
Mass: 375 kg
Power: 335 W average

Data Products

Time Resolution: Down to 100 ns
Data Modes: Event, Binned
Telemetry Rate: ~1 Mbps science
Archive: HEASARC
Data Rights: Public after 1 year
File Format: FITS

Key Scientific Capabilities

Precision Timing

Microsecond-level timing precision enables detailed studies of neutron star rotation, pulsations, and timing noise. Critical for radius measurements through pulse profile modeling.

High-Resolution Spectroscopy

Superior energy resolution allows detailed studies of emission lines, absorption features, and continuum spectra from neutron star atmospheres and accretion flows.

Flexible Pointing

ISS-based platform provides access to ~80% of the sky, with flexible scheduling allowing rapid response to transient events and coordinated multi-wavelength campaigns.

Long-Term Monitoring

Continuous operation enables long-term monitoring of neutron star systems, studying orbital evolution, accretion state changes, and long-term timing stability.

High Count Rates

Large effective area and low background enable studies of bright X-ray sources with unprecedented statistics, crucial for detailed timing and spectral analysis.

Low Background

Excellent background rejection through coincidence shielding and energy discrimination enables sensitive observations of faint sources and detailed background modeling.

Mission Team & Partners

Lead Institution

NASA Goddard Space Flight Center leads the NICER mission, providing project management, instrument development, mission operations, and data processing.

Principal Investigator

Dr. Keith Gendreau serves as the Principal Investigator, leading the science team and overseeing mission operations and scientific objectives.

International Partners

Collaboration with institutions worldwide, including universities in the US, Canada, Europe, and Asia, contributing to instrument development and scientific analysis.

Industry Partners

Partnership with aerospace and technology companies for instrument fabrication, testing, and integration, ensuring mission success and reliability.
